Welcome to Instrumetriq

See what people on X (Twitter) are actually saying about any crypto coin, while you're on your trading page.

  • Latest update: how many accounts posted in the most recent update window, against this coin's own usual rate. It carries the time it was collected, so you always know how fresh it is.
  • Last 24 hours: tone, topics, who's posting and volume. A single update is often only a handful of posts, too few to read anything into. A day is enough to be meaningful.
  • Discussed: the topics coming up: hype, fear, scam talk, memes.
  • Context-aware: opens to the coin you're looking at on Binance, TradingView, or Bybit.

The chart works the same way: switch between per-update, daily and weekly bars with the buttons above it.

This is not a trading signal. Instrumetriq describes social conversation only. It does not predict, forecast, or recommend any action. Always do your own research.

Data updates roughly once an hour. Free tier: 2 coins. Pro: all 270+ coins.

How it works

What this is

Instrumetriq watches what people post on X about 270+ crypto coins and describes the conversation: how many accounts are talking, how the tone splits, and what topics come up. It describes what is being said. It does not tell you what a price will do.

Two speeds, and why

The card is split. Latest update is the most recent collection window, and shows how long ago it ran. Last 24 hours is everything below the divider.

We would show everything live if the data supported it, but it does not. A single update window is often only a handful of posts for a given coin, too few to read a tone from without one loud post swinging it. Counting accounts works fine on a small sample, so attention is live; tone, topics and reach need a day to mean anything.

The chart follows the same idea: switch it to per-update bars to see the freshest detail, or leave it on days for the shape over time.

Attention

The headline number on each coin. We count how many distinct accounts posted about it in the latest update window, then compare that to the median of its own recent windows.

A coin at 1× is posting at its usual rate. At 6× or more we mark it unusual attention, which happens to roughly one coin in a hundred at any moment. That rarity is the point: if the badge appeared constantly it would tell you nothing.

We count accounts rather than posts on purpose. Fifty posts from one person is one person; five posts from five people is a conversation.

Tone of posts

Every post is read by two language models. We report the split, what share came out positive, neutral and negative, and a plain label for that mix.

Tone describes the language people used. It is not a direction, a target or a suggestion. A coin can be discussed in overwhelmingly positive terms while its price falls, and often is.

We only show tone when at least five posts were scored. Below that there is nothing meaningful to average.

How clear-cut

How consistently our scoring landed. Clear means the models were confident and aligned. Mixed means confidence varied widely across posts. Unclear means our two models disagreed on a meaningful share of them. It tells you how much weight the tone reading deserves.

It needs the same five scored posts that tone does, but it is measured separately, so it can be available when tone is not.

Discussed

Topics drawn from a curated crypto vocabulary, hype, fear, scam talk, memes, and general positive or negative language, with the words that triggered them.

This is deliberately kept separate from tone, and the two can look like they disagree. A conversation can be broadly positive while people also discuss risks; the words “not a scam” contain the word scam. Roughly two thirds of posts contain none of this vocabulary at all, so we show how many posts the topics were drawn from: it is the same figure as the Posts row, and hovering Discussed repeats it. The number beside each topic is how many matching words were found rather than how many posts, since one post can contribute several.

Only curated vocabulary appears here. We never surface raw hashtags, @mentions or links.

Who’s posting

Distinct accounts over the last 24 hours, and at least how many carried a verified badge. The verified figure is written as “60+” because verified accounts cannot be matched up across separate update windows: it is the count from the busiest single window, so the true figure over the day may be higher.

We never publish account names, account IDs or post IDs, only counts and aggregates.

The chart

Each bar shows how many distinct accounts posted, and its colour shows the tone of those posts, green when more positive than this coin’s own baseline, red when more negative, grey when near it or when there is too little to say.

The three buttons switch between one bar per update, per day and per week. Day is the default: most coins are too quiet for a single update window to say much, but busy enough over a full day.

Bar height is the average across the windows in which a coin was actually being discussed, so a coin talked about in two windows is not flattened by the twenty-five quiet ones around it. How often it was discussed is shown by how many bars there are.

The scale is logarithmic. A viral moment can be thousands of times a quiet stretch, and on a plain scale everything else would vanish.

Market conversation

At the top of the popup, how positive the conversation is across every tracked coin today, compared with the last 30 days. It is a daily average rather than a live reading. Hour to hour it is mostly noise, and it typically moves about a point a day.

Where the numbers come from

Posts are collected continuously and processed in full sweeps of every coin, paced to stay within the API limits of the sources we read. All coins update together, so a reading over an hour old is normal. Price and volume come from Binance.

Limits worth knowing

  • Only English-language posts on X. Discussion elsewhere is invisible to us.
  • Automated and promotional posts are filtered, but no filter is perfect.
  • Quiet coins genuinely have little to report. We say so rather than inventing detail.
  • Nothing here is a price prediction, and none of it should be read as one.

This is not a trading signal. Instrumetriq describes social conversation only. It does not predict, forecast, or recommend any action, and nothing in it is financial advice. Data may be delayed.
